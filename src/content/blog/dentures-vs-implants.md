When teeth are missing, the two main roads are removable dentures or fixed implants — and patients often arrive convinced one of them is "obviously" better. The real answer depends on your mouth, your health, and your priorities. Here's the comparison we walk patients through.

## Chewing and comfort

**Implants** win on function: they're fixed in bone, so you bite with force close to natural teeth — apples, corn, rotis without strategy. **Dentures** restore respectable chewing, but a full lower denture in particular rests on a moving foundation of gum and muscle; tougher foods take technique and patience.

## Bone health — the quiet difference

This one matters more over the years. Jawbone stays strong because tooth roots stimulate it. **Implants provide that stimulation; dentures don't.** Under a denture, the ridge of bone slowly shrinks — which is why dentures loosen over time and faces can look "collapsed" after many years. An implant is the only replacement that actively preserves bone.

## Daily life and maintenance

Dentures come out at night and get cleaned separately; they're simple but they're an *appliance*. Implants are brushed and flossed like your own teeth — nothing to remove, nothing to soak, just [regular cleaning visits](/treatments/teeth-cleaning/) like everyone else.

## The cost logic

Dentures cost significantly less upfront — that's real and it matters. The fuller picture: dentures typically need relining as the bone changes and remaking every 5–8 years, while a well-maintained implant is usually a one-time investment. Over 15–20 years, the gap narrows considerably. Neither choice is wrong; they're different payment shapes for different budgets.

## Who suits what

**Dentures make sense when:** many or all teeth are missing and budget is the deciding factor, when health conditions make surgery unwise, or when bone volume is very limited and grafting isn't an option.

**Implants make sense when:** you want fixed teeth, your bone and general health allow it (most healthy adults qualify — there's no age cutoff), and you're ready for a longer one-time investment.

## The middle path most people don't know about

**Implant-supported dentures.** Two to four implants hold a removable denture firmly in place with press-stud-like attachments. It transforms the loose-lower-denture experience at a fraction of full-arch implant cost — and it's often the sweet spot for long-time denture wearers who are tired of movement but don't want a full implant bridge.
