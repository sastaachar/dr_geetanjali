The honest answer: with good placement and good care, a dental implant routinely lasts **15–25 years, and very often a lifetime**. Long-term studies consistently show success rates above 90–95% at the 10-year mark. But averages hide the interesting part — *what makes the difference between an implant that lasts five years and one that lasts fifty.*

## The implant vs. the crown

An implant has two parts with different lifespans:

- **The implant itself** — the titanium post fused with your jawbone. This is the part that can last a lifetime. Once bone has grown onto it (osseointegration), it's remarkably stable.
- **The crown on top** — like any tooth crown, it experiences years of chewing. Crowns typically serve 10–15 years before wear or chipping calls for a replacement. Replacing a crown is straightforward and doesn't disturb the implant below.

So "my implant needed work after 12 years" usually means a new crown — not a new implant.

## What actually makes implants fail

**Gum disease around the implant.** The number one long-term threat. Plaque doesn't care that the tooth is titanium — untreated, it inflames the gum and then the bone holding the implant (*peri-implantitis*). This is why implant patients need the same cleaning discipline as everyone else, arguably more.

**Smoking.** It impairs the blood supply the healing bone depends on, and doubles down on gum disease risk. Smokers' implants fail measurably more often.

**Uncontrolled diabetes.** Well-controlled diabetes is compatible with implants; uncontrolled blood sugar impairs healing.

**Poor planning.** An implant placed without proper assessment of bone volume and bite forces struggles from day one. This part you can't control after the fact — which is why choosing where you get the implant matters more than any aftercare tip.

## What you control

The maintenance routine is unglamorous and effective: brush twice daily, clean between teeth, and keep six-monthly [professional cleaning visits](/treatments/teeth-cleaning/) where we also check the gum around the implant. That's it. No special gadgets required.

## The bottom line

An implant is the closest dentistry gets to a permanent tooth replacement — and it's a partnership. We plan and place it right; you keep it clean; it keeps working.
