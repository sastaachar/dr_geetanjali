Tooth pain has a talent for starting at 11 pm. Until you can reach a dentist, here's what actually helps, what makes things worse, and how to tell an "endure till morning" situation from a "get help now" one.

## What actually helps

**A painkiller, taken correctly.** An over-the-counter painkiller like paracetamol or ibuprofen (if you have no condition that rules it out — follow the label, and check with a pharmacist if unsure) is the most effective thing in the house. Take it on schedule rather than waiting for the pain to peak.

**Keep your head elevated.** Lying flat increases blood pressure in the tooth's inflamed nerve — it's why toothaches feel worse at night. Prop yourself up with an extra pillow; it genuinely helps.

**Cold compress on the cheek.** For swelling or injury, a cloth-wrapped ice pack against the cheek in 15-minute intervals numbs the area and slows the swelling.

**Warm salt-water rinses.** Half a teaspoon of salt in warm water, gently swished — soothing for gum-related pain and helps flush food from a cavity.

**Clean the area.** Surprisingly often, severe pain is food packed hard into a cavity or between teeth. Gentle brushing and flossing around the sore tooth sometimes brings real relief.

## What makes it worse

- **Aspirin on the gum.** The old remedy of holding an aspirin against the tooth *burns the gum tissue* — it's acid on skin. Painkillers work from the stomach, not by contact.
- **Heat packs on the face.** Heat feels nice for a moment but expands swelling and spreads infection. Cold, not hot.
- **Very hot, cold or sweet food.** An inflamed nerve overreacts to everything. Lukewarm and bland until you're seen.
- **Clove oil overdone.** A drop on the tooth can dull pain briefly and is fine as a stopgap; soaking the area repeatedly irritates the tissue.

## What can't wait till morning

Most toothaches can hold with painkillers until the next day's appointment. These cannot:

- **Facial swelling that is spreading** — towards the eye or down the neck
- **Fever with the toothache**
- **Difficulty swallowing or opening your mouth**
- **A knocked-out permanent tooth** — keep it in milk, get to a dentist within the hour

These are signs of infection spreading beyond the tooth. Don't wait these out.

## The part nobody wants to hear

Pain that vanishes on its own didn't get better — a nerve that dies stops reporting. The infection continues silently until it returns as a swelling. A toothache severe enough to keep you up has earned an examination, pain or no pain the next morning.
