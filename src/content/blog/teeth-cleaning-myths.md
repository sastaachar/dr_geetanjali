Professional cleaning is the simplest, most protective treatment in dentistry — yet it's surrounded by more myths than any other. These are the five we hear most often at the clinic, and what's actually true.

## Myth 1: "Scaling loosens your teeth"

The most stubborn myth of all. Scaling removes **tartar** — hardened plaque — from tooth surfaces and from under the gumline. It does not touch the bone or ligaments that hold teeth in place.

Where does the myth come from? When heavy tartar has been building for years, it can act like cement packed around already-loose teeth. Remove it, and the looseness that gum disease *already caused* becomes noticeable. The cleaning revealed the damage; it didn't cause it. Ironically, regular cleaning is exactly what would have prevented that looseness.

## Myth 2: "Scaling scrapes away enamel"

Ultrasonic scalers work by vibration, not grinding. They knock tartar off the surface without cutting into enamel — which is, for reference, the hardest substance in the human body. A cleaning twice a year does no measurable harm to enamel over a lifetime.

## Myth 3: "Gaps appear between teeth after cleaning"

Same story as Myth 1: those spaces were there, filled with tartar. Once it's removed, you feel gaps that gum recession had already created. Within days the gums typically tighten back around clean tooth surfaces, and the "gaps" feel smaller.

## Myth 4: "If I brush well, I never need professional cleaning"

Excellent brushing removes plaque — but any plaque that survives 48 hours starts hardening into tartar, and **no toothbrush can remove tartar**. Even dentists get their own teeth professionally cleaned. Brushing and cleaning visits are teammates, not substitutes.

## Myth 5: "Cleaning makes teeth sensitive forever"

Some sensitivity for a day or two after a deep cleaning is common, especially if gums were inflamed — exposed surfaces are suddenly clean and responsive. It settles quickly. Permanent sensitivity from routine scaling is not a thing; untreated gum disease causing recession, however, very much is.

## What a cleaning visit actually looks like

Fifteen to thirty minutes: an examination, ultrasonic scaling, polishing, and honest advice about anything we noticed. Most patients describe it as ticklish rather than painful — and everyone leaves with that glassy-smooth feeling you can't get from a brush.
